## 🔄 1. Customer Lifecycle

The Customer Lifecycle represents the stages customers move through while building a relationship with a business.

```text
Awareness
    ↓
Acquisition
    ↓
Engagement
    ↓
Retention
    ↓
Advocacy
```

### Awareness

The customer discovers the brand or product.

### Acquisition

The customer makes an initial conversion or purchase.

### Engagement

The customer continues interacting with the business.

### Retention

The customer continues purchasing or using the service.

### Advocacy

Satisfied customers may recommend the business to others.

## 📐 2. Customer Metrics & Formulas

### Customer Acquisition Rate

Measures the proportion of customers acquired during the period.

```text
Customer Acquisition Rate =
(New Customers / Total Customers) × 100
```

### Customer Retention Rate

Measures how effectively existing customers are retained.

```text
Retention Rate =
((Customers at End - New Customers)
 / Customers at Start) × 100
```

### Customer Churn Rate

Measures the percentage of customers lost during a period.

```text
Churn Rate =
(Customers Lost / Customers at Start) × 100
```

### Customer Lifetime Value — Simplified

Estimates the value generated by a customer throughout the relationship.

```text
CLV =
Average Purchase Value
× Purchase Frequency
× Customer Lifespan
```

### Repeat Purchase Rate

Measures the percentage of customers who purchased more than once.

```text
Repeat Purchase Rate =
(Repeat Customers / Total Customers) × 100
```

## 👥 3. Customer Segmentation

Customer Segmentation groups customers with similar characteristics so businesses can better understand behavior and personalize strategies.

Common segmentation approaches include:

* **Demographic** — age, income, occupation, etc.
* **Behavioral** — purchases, engagement, product usage
* **Geographic** — location
* **Psychographic** — interests, lifestyle, preferences
* **Value-Based** — customer value or profitability

Instead of treating every customer identically, segmentation helps businesses develop more relevant strategies for different groups.

## 💎 4. RFM Analysis

**RFM = Recency + Frequency + Monetary**

RFM Analysis is a customer segmentation technique based on purchasing behavior.

### Recency (R)

How recently did the customer purchase?

```text
Recency =
Analysis Date - Last Purchase Date
```

Lower recency generally indicates a more recent customer interaction.

### Frequency (F)

How often does the customer purchase?

```text
Frequency =
Total Number of Purchases
```

### Monetary (M)

How much has the customer spent?

```text
Monetary =
SUM(Customer Purchase Amount)
```

### RFM Score

Customers can receive scores for each dimension.

```text
RFM Score = R Score + F Score + M Score
```

or be represented as a combined code such as:

```text
555 → Strong scores across R, F and M
```

RFM can support segments such as:

**Champions | Loyal Customers | Potential Loyalists | New Customers | At Risk | Hibernating**

## 📅 5. Cohort Analysis

Cohort Analysis groups customers using a common characteristic or starting event and tracks their behavior over time.

Example:

```text
January Cohort → Customers acquired in January
February Cohort → Customers acquired in February
March Cohort → Customers acquired in March
```

A retention matrix can then compare:

```text
Cohort | M0  | M1 | M2 | M3
Jan    | 100 | 72 | 60 | 52
Feb    | 100 | 68 | 58 | 50
Mar    | 100 | 70 | 62 | 55
```

This helps investigate questions such as:

* Which cohorts retain customers longer?
* When does retention begin declining?
* Are newer cohorts performing better?
* Did certain campaigns acquire stronger customers?

# 🛒 Hands-On Case Study: Customer Purchase Analysis

## Business Objective

Analyze customer purchase behavior to identify high-value customers, understand repeat purchasing, evaluate retention and churn, and uncover opportunities for improving customer relationships.

## Business Questions

1. Who are our most valuable customers?
2. Which segments generate the most revenue?
3. How many customers purchase repeatedly?
4. Which customers or segments are at risk?
5. Where are customers being lost?
6. How does retention differ across cohorts?
7. What actions could improve retention?

## 📊 Additional Customer KPIs

### Average Order Value

```text
AOV = Total Revenue / Total Orders
```

### Purchase Frequency

```text
Purchase Frequency =
Total Orders / Unique Customers
```

### Revenue per Customer

```text
Revenue per Customer =
Total Revenue / Total Customers
```

## 💡 Key Takeaway

The biggest lesson from today's learning is that **Customer Analytics isn't simply about counting customers or reporting revenue.**

It helps connect:

```text
Customer Data
      ↓
Customer Behavior
      ↓
Patterns & Segments
      ↓
Business Insights
      ↓
Customer Strategy
      ↓
Business Impact
```

A strong analysis should help answer:

> **Who are our customers, how are they behaving, which relationships create value, where are we losing customers, and what can the business do about it?**

The dashboard communicates the findings, but **the business decision is where analytics creates value.**
