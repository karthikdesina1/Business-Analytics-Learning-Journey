# Financial & Revenue Analytics

## Overview

Financial and Revenue Analytics helps businesses understand not only how much money they generate, but also how efficiently they convert revenue into profit, acquire customers, and generate returns from investments.

This section covers the most important financial and business metrics used in analytics.

---

## 1. Revenue

Revenue is the total income generated from selling products or services.

**Formula:**

```text
Revenue = Price × Quantity Sold
```

Example:

```text
$100 × 500 units = $50,000 Revenue
```

---

## 2. Gross Profit

Gross Profit shows how much money remains after subtracting direct production costs.

```text
Gross Profit = Revenue − Cost of Goods Sold
```

Example:

```text
Revenue = $100,000
COGS = $60,000

Gross Profit = $40,000
```

---

## 3. Net Profit

Net Profit represents the final profit after all expenses.

```text
Net Profit = Total Revenue − Total Expenses
```

Expenses may include:

* COGS
* Salaries
* Rent
* Marketing
* Interest
* Taxes
* Operating expenses

---

## 4. Profit Margin %

Profit Margin helps measure profitability relative to revenue.

```text
Net Profit Margin = (Net Profit / Revenue) × 100
```

Example:

```text
Net Profit = $20,000
Revenue = $100,000

Margin = 20%
```

---

## 5. EBITDA

EBITDA stands for:

**Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation and Amortization**

```text
EBITDA =
Net Income
+ Interest
+ Taxes
+ Depreciation
+ Amortization
```

It is commonly used to evaluate operating performance.

---

## 6. ROI — Return on Investment

ROI measures the return generated from an investment.

```text
ROI =
(Return − Investment Cost)
--------------------------
Investment Cost
× 100
```

Example:

```text
Investment = $10,000
Return = $13,000

ROI = 30%
```

---

## 7. ROAS — Return on Ad Spend

ROAS measures advertising efficiency.

```text
ROAS = Revenue Generated from Ads / Advertising Cost
```

Example:

```text
Ad Revenue = $20,000
Ad Spend = $5,000

ROAS = 4x
```

This means every $1 spent on advertising generated $4 in revenue.

---

## 8. CAC — Customer Acquisition Cost

CAC measures the cost of acquiring one new customer.

```text
CAC =
Total Sales & Marketing Cost
----------------------------
New Customers Acquired
```

Example:

```text
Marketing Cost = $10,000
New Customers = 200

CAC = $50
```

---

## 9. LTV — Customer Lifetime Value

LTV estimates how much revenue a customer may generate during the entire relationship with a business.

A simple formula is:

```text
LTV =
Average Purchase Value
× Purchase Frequency
× Customer Lifetime
```

Example:

```text
Average Purchase = $100
Purchases per Year = 4
Customer Lifetime = 3 Years

LTV = $1,200
```

A useful relationship to study is:

```text
LTV vs CAC
```

If customer acquisition cost is too high compared with customer value, growth may become difficult to sustain.

---

## 10. Break-even Analysis

Break-even analysis identifies the point where:

```text
Total Revenue = Total Cost
```

### Break-even Units

```text
Break-even Units =
Fixed Costs
------------------------------
Selling Price − Variable Cost
```

Example:

```text
Fixed Cost = $50,000
Price = $100
Variable Cost = $60

Break-even Units = 1,250
```

The company must sell 1,250 units before it begins generating profit.

# How the Metrics Connect

```text
Sales
  ↓
Revenue
  ↓
Gross Profit
  ↓
Net Profit
  ↓
Margin %
  ↓
Profitability
```

Marketing analysis adds another layer:

```text
Marketing Spend
      ↓
     CAC
      ↓
Customers Acquired
      ↓
     LTV
      ↓
ROAS / ROI
```

# Example Business Questions

These metrics help answer questions such as:

* Is revenue growing?
* Is the company actually profitable?
* Which product generates the highest margin?
* Is advertising generating enough revenue?
* How expensive is customer acquisition?
* Is customer lifetime value higher than CAC?
* How long will it take to break even?
* Are investments generating acceptable returns?

---

# Key Analytical Insight

High revenue does not automatically mean strong business performance.

For example:

```text
High Revenue
+ High Costs
+ High CAC
+ Low Margin
= Weak Profitability
```

A stronger business picture requires analyzing multiple metrics together.

```text
Revenue
   ↓
Profitability
   ↓
Efficiency
   ↓
Customer Economics
   ↓
Investment Returns
   ↓
Business Decisions
```

## Learning Goal

The objective is to understand how analysts move from financial numbers to business decisions.

Instead of only reporting:

> “Revenue increased.”

The next step is to ask:

> Did profit increase?
> Did margins improve?
> Was marketing efficient?
> Did customer value exceed acquisition cost?
> Is the growth sustainable?

That is where Financial & Revenue Analytics becomes valuable.
