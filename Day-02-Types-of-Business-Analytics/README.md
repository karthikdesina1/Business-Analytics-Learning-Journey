# 📊 Business Analytics — Types of Analytics & Analytics Lifecycle
📌 Today's Learning
Today, I explored the four major types of Business Analytics and how organizations can use them to move from historical data toward better business decisions.

The key concepts covered were:
* Descriptive Analytics
* Diagnostic Analytics
* Predictive Analytics
* Prescriptive Analytics
* Real-world application of the four analytics types
* Analytics Lifecycle
* How analysts convert business problems into actionable insights
---
🔹 1. Descriptive Analytics

#What happened?

Descriptive Analytics focuses on understanding historical business performance.
It summarizes existing data using:

* Reports
* Dashboards
* KPIs
* Trends
* Charts and visualizations

Example:
An e-commerce company generated $1.2M in sales last quarter, representing a 10% increase compared with the previous quarter.

The objective is to clearly understand what has already happened.
---
🔍 2. Diagnostic Analytics

#Why did it happen?
Diagnostic Analytics investigates the reasons behind an observed result.

Common techniques include:
* Drill-down analysis
* Data segmentation
* Correlation analysis
* Trend comparison
* Root-cause analysis

Example:
Further analysis shows that sales growth was associated with:

* 25% higher website traffic
* A successful seasonal campaign
* Increased mobile conversions

Instead of simply reporting growth, Diagnostic Analytics helps investigate what contributed to it.
---
🔮 3. Predictive Analytics

#What is likely to happen?

Predictive Analytics uses historical data and patterns to estimate potential future outcomes.

It may involve:
* Statistical analysis
* Forecasting
* Regression
* Machine Learning
* Trend analysis

Example:
Based on historical performance and current trends, the company estimates that sales could grow by 15% next quarter, particularly in the mobile segment.
Predictive Analytics helps businesses prepare for possible future scenarios.
---
🎯 4. Prescriptive Analytics

#What should we do?
Prescriptive Analytics focuses on determining potential actions based on available data and predictions.
Techniques can include:
* Optimization
* Simulation
* Scenario analysis
* AI/ML
* Decision models

Example:
Based on expected demand, the company could evaluate actions such as:

* Increasing investment in high-performing marketing channels
* Improving mobile product recommendations
* Testing similar seasonal campaigns
* Allocating inventory toward expected demand

The goal is to move from insight toward informed action.

🔄 Analytics Lifecycle
Analytics is not only about analyzing a dataset. It begins with understanding the business problem and continues through implementation and measurement.

```text
Define Business Problem
        ↓
Collect Data
        ↓
Prepare & Clean Data
        ↓
Analyze Data
        ↓
Generate Insights
        ↓
Communicate Findings
        ↓
Take Action
        ↓
Measure Results & Improve
        ↺
```
### 1️⃣ Define the Problem

Understand the business objective and identify the questions that need to be answered.

### 2️⃣ Collect Data

Gather relevant information from databases, spreadsheets, applications, APIs, surveys, or other sources.

### 3️⃣ Prepare Data

Clean, transform, validate, and organize the data before analysis.

### 4️⃣ Analyze Data

Apply appropriate descriptive, diagnostic, predictive, or prescriptive techniques.

### 5️⃣ Communicate Insights

Present findings using dashboards, reports, visualizations, and data storytelling.

### 6️⃣ Take Action

Translate analytical findings into business decisions or recommendations.

### 7️⃣ Measure & Improve

Evaluate the results and use new information to improve future decisions.

# 💡 Key Takeaways

The biggest lesson from today's learning is that Business Analytics is not just about tools or dashboards.

It connects:
Business Problem → Data → Analysis → Insight → Recommendation → Action → Impact

Each type of analytics answers a different question:

| Analytics Type  | Business Question         |
| --------------- | ------------------------- |
| 📊 Descriptive  | What happened?            |
| 🔍 Diagnostic   | Why did it happen?        |
| 🔮 Predictive   | What is likely to happen? |
| 🎯 Prescriptive | What should we do?        |

A strong analyst should not stop after finding a number or building a visualization.

The real objective is to understand what the data means for the business and how those insights can support better decisions.
